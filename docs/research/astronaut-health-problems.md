## (tail of a headaches / CO2 section)

- Headaches and vision changes (SANS, Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome) are the most common symptoms in spaceflight (NASA OCHMO).
- In one study, 22 of 24 ISS astronauts reported headaches. Most were tension-type, and they recurred throughout the mission.
- Fluid shift toward the head causes constant pressure (Scott Kelly: body adapted, but never fully).

## 7. Workload, tedium, and procedures

- Astronaut diaries (Jack Stuster's studies) describe slow, repetitive tasks. In one example, a 55-step procedure was given only 30 minutes.
- A typical ISS workday runs about 16 hours, from wake-up to sleep.
- Skylab 4 (1973): an overpacked schedule led to fatigue, frustration, and a crew work slowdown.
- Even private Axiom-1 crew described the pace as "frenetic".

Design implication: any tool that needs a lot of manual input will not be used. Use passive data collection and short check-ins.

## 8. Isolation, interpersonal friction, and conflict with Mission Control

- Cosmonauts and astronauts agreed that the hardest interpersonal problems were between crew and Mission Control, not inside the crew (Safe Passage).
- Diaries show irritation from close quarters, and resentment when one crewmember's activities added to others' workload.
- Stuster estimates a ~99% chance that someone on a Mars mission will develop serious behavioral or psychological issues.
- His recommendations include giving astronauts more influence over their schedules, and moving ground control from directing the crew to consulting it.
- Being away from family is consistently named as one of the hardest parts.

Design implication: support autonomy and self-management. Avoid a "surveillance" feel.

## Problem -> opportunity summary

| Real problem | What a solution could do |
|---|---|
| Hiding symptoms out of fear of grounding | Private-by-default data; astronaut decides what to share |
| No access to own medical data | Personal history, trends, and baselines on board |
| No real-time Earth support | Offline-first evaluation + clear next steps |
| Whole crew sleep-deprived/medicated | Crew-level readiness view |
| CO2/environment affects how they feel | Correlate symptoms with environment data |
| No time, tedious procedures | Passive collection, 30-second check-ins |
| Conflict with ground, loss of autonomy | Self-management tools, not surveillance |
